# Slop Lexicon — Reference

Complete word and phrase lists for Section 1 (Vocabulary Red Flags).

## Slop Lexicon (Core)

Words and phrases that, in concentration, correlate with low-substance
or AI-processed text:

- "delve"
- "dive into"
- "unpack"
- "landscape"
- "realm"
- "paradigm shift"
- "game-changer"
- "cutting-edge"
- "robust"
- "streamline"
- "holistic"
- "synergy"
- "leverage" (as verb)
- "foster"
- "tapestry"
- "multifaceted"
- "pivotal"
- "crucial"
- "navigate" (metaphorical)
- "at the heart of"
- "in today's [adjective] world"

## Padding Connectors

Connectors that fill space without expressing a logical relationship:

- "moreover"
- "furthermore"
- "additionally"
- "it's worth noting"
- "it's important to note"
- "needless to say" (then why say it?)

## Sycophantic Openers

Sentences whose function is to flatter the reader before content begins:

- "Absolutely!"
- "Great question!"
- "That's a really interesting point!"
- Any sentence that praises the reader or interlocutor before answering.

## Empty Intensifiers

Removable without changing the sentence's meaning:

- "truly"
- "incredibly"
- "remarkably"
- "genuinely"
- "very"

**Test:** Delete the intensifier. Does the sentence's meaning change? If
not, the word was filler.

## AI Verbal Tics

Recognizable patterns even when surrounding content is strong:

- "This is not an exotic scenario"
- "The critical point:"
- "What can be said with confidence is"

These are not universal — they shift over time as models retrain and
fine-tune.

## Temporal Note

The vocabulary signals documented in peer-reviewed research (Kobak et al.
2025, Liang et al. 2025, Juzek & Ward 2025) reflect language use patterns
as of 2024–2025. As models update and language communities adapt, Section 1
vocabulary will become less diagnostic. Substance diagnostics (Tier 2) are
slower to become obsolete.

## Formatting Discipline (Appendix)

Not slop detectors per se, but formatting habits that correlate with
low-substance prose:

- **Bold text** should be rare and functional — emphasis on a keyword, a
  defined term, a critical warning. Not decorating every other phrase.
- **Headers** exist because structure requires them, not because they
  "look professional." Three sentences do not need a header.
- **Bullets** are for genuinely list-shaped information with no internal
  logical ordering. If content works as prose, write prose.
- **Tables** are for structured comparison across consistent dimensions.
  Not for forcing prose into a grid.
